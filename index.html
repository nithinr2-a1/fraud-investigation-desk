How the score is computed (trained logistic regression)

The risk score is the output of a logistic regression model trained in scikit-learn on a synthetic 6,000-claim dataset (no real claims data was used or required). It is not a fixed checklist — the weight on every signal below was learned from data, not hand-set.

ModelTest accuracyPrecisionRecallROC-AUC
Logistic Regression (deployed)————

For each claim, eleven raw and engineered features (claim-to-limit ratio, claim-to-premium ratio, policy age, prior claims, reporting delay, police report status, round-number amount, and claim type) are standardized using the training set's mean and standard deviation, combined with the model's learned coefficients, and passed through a sigmoid to produce a fraud probability — entirely in your browser, nothing is sent to a server.

score = sigmoid( Σ coefficient × (feature − mean) / scale + intercept )

Claims scoring 65+ are flagged High, 35–64 Medium, below that Low. The detail panel shows each claim's top contributing features, signed by whether they pushed the score up or down. Missing fields in an uploaded CSV are imputed at the training-set average (zero contribution) rather than guessed, so scores degrade gracefully on incomplete data. Full training methodology, data generation process, and evaluation charts are documented in the companion report.

Feature importance & model comparison

A random forest was trained as a benchmark; it performed comparably (ROC-AUC ≈0.85 vs. 0.86 for logistic regression), suggesting the relationship is close to linear once ratios are computed — so the simpler, fully-explainable model was deployed. Both models agree on the dominant signals: claim-to-limit ratio, early-policy claims, and prior claims by the same claimant.
